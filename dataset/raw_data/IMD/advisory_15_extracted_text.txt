Sub: Very Severe Cyclonic Storm ―Arnab‖ [Pronunciation: Ornab] over Westcentral Bay of 
Bengal off Andhra Pradesh coast [Post Landfall Outlook for Andhra Pradesh and adjoining 
South Odisha coasts—Red Message] 
 
The very severe cyclonic storm “Arnab” [Pronunciation :Ornab] over Westcentral Bay of Bengal off 
Andhra Pradesh coast  moved nearly west-northwestwards with a speed of 22 kmph duri ng past 6 
hours, and lay centered at 1800 UTC of 27th October, 2026 over the same region , near latitude 
16.3°N and longitude 82.1°E, about 50 km east -southeast of Narsapur ( 43187), 70 km south of 
Kakinada ( 43189), 100 k m east of Machilipatnam ( 43185), 180 km east -northeast of Bapatla 
(43220), and  200 km south-southwest of Vishakhapatnam (43149), 
  
It is very likely to recurve northwestwards and cross Andhra Pradesh & Yanam coasts between 
Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, close to south of Yanam as a very severe cyclonic storm with the 
maximum sustained wind speed of 125 -135 kmph gusting to 150 kmph around 2100 UTC of 27th 
October, 2026.

Very severe cyclonic storm “Arnab” is continuing to interact  with the land surface and the spiral band of 
northern sector has further entered into the land. Centre of Severe cyclonic storm is very close to the coast 
line and the landfall process will commence during next 1 -2 hours. It is likely to make landfall dur ing early 
hours (2130-2330 UTC) of 27th October. 
 
The system is under continuous surveillance of the Doppler Weat her Radar at 
Machillipatnam. 
 
As per latest DWR Machilipatnam imagery at 1 800 UTC, there is a sig nificant reduction in 
convection in wall cloud region especially in eastern and northern sector. Eye is not seen  at 1800 
UTC. The outer spiral ba nd in the northern sector is interacting with the land surface. All these 
signal further weakening of the system.  
 
The i ntensity of the system is characte rized as T4.0. Associated scattered to broken, low and 
medium clouds with embedded intense to very intense convection lay over the northwest, central 
& south Bay of Bengal and North Coastal Andhra Pradesh, southeast Odisha, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, 
Jharkhand, Gangetic West Bengal, East Madhya Pradesh,  and East Telangana. Minimum Cloud 
Top Temperature (CTT) had been -70 to -90°C.  Moderate to intense convection also prevail ed 
over North east Bay of Bengal and west Telangana, rest Bihar and Southeast Uttar Prades h, 
Rayalaseema, West Telangana (Minimum Cloud Top Temperature (CTT) was -40 to -70°C). The 
multi-satellite imagery indicates stronger winds in the northwest sector.  
 
The estimated central pressure is 9 84 hPa and maximum sustained wind speed  (MSW) is 75 kt 
gusting to 85 kt.  
 
Visakhapatnam (43149) reported mean s ea level pressure (MSLP) of 100 5.2 hPa, maximum 
sustained wind speed (MSW) as 20°/ 24 kt, P24 as -3.5 hPa. Tuni reported MSLP as of 1003.0 
hPa, MSW as 50°/ 8 kt, P24 as -5.8 hPa. Kakinada reported MSLP as of 1000.0 hPa, MSW as 
70°/ 8 kt, P24 as -9.2 hPa. Narsapur reported MSLP as of 985.4 hPa, MSW as 290°/ 35 kt, P24 as 
-23.6 hPa.

Storm surge of 1.5 to 2.5 m height above astronomical tide is very likely to inundate low lying 
areas of Andhra Prad esh during the time of landfall. The maximum peak surge is expected over 
near Kakinada and Yanam (Puducherry).  Surge height of 2 -3.5 m is expected along the Godavari 
river mouth. (Figure attached). 
 
 
Sea Condition: 
 
Sea condition is likely to be Phenomenal till landfall (forenoon of 28th October). After landfall, it 
would start improving becoming very high to high by evening of 28th October and improve further 
becoming very rough to rough by morning of 29th October and further improve gradually 
thereafter. 
 
Remarks – 
The guidance from various numerical models indicates that the Madden Julian Oscillation (MJO) is 
currently in Phase 3 with amplitude more than 1. It is likely to continue in same phase during next 
5 days with amplitude more than 1. Thus, MJO is favorable for enhancement of convective activity 
and further intensification of “Arnab” till landfall over the Bay of Bengal (BoB). 
  
The guidance from the NCICS model indicates  prevalence of westerly wind anomaly (7 -9mps) 
along with simultaneous occurrence of Equatorial Rossby wave (ERW),   Kelvin wave (KW) & 
Madden Julian Os cillation ( MJO) wave over south and adjoining central BoB and easterly wind 
anomaly (7-9 mps) over north BoB. These features indicate a highly favorable environment for its 
further intensification of system till 27th Oct.  
 
The Sea surface temperature is 30°C over eastcentral and adjoining Westcentral BoB and around  
29°C along the east coast of India.  The ocean heat content is around 80 -100 KJ/cm 2 over most 
parts of Bay of Bengal with 100 -120 KJ/cm 2 over some pockets over southwest and adjoining 
westcentral Bay of Bengal. 
 
The positive low -level vorticity is around 200 X10-6 s-1 to the south of system area  with vertical 
extension upto 200 hPa. Another zone of positive vorticity of 100 X10-6 s-1 is seen to its southeast, 
in association with the upper air cycloni c circulation over southwe st BoB. The low level vorticity is 
circularly oriented and is interacting with the vorticity associated with the upper air cyclonic 
circulation over southwest Bay of Bengal. The low level convergence has decreased and is around 
30X10-6 s-1 near the east of system centre. It is extending upto southeast Bay of Bengal . Upper-
level divergence is the same and is around 30X10-6 s-1 to the northeast of system centre  and 
another zone to its southeast (around  30X10-6 s-1). Mid-level w ind-shear (500-850 hpa)  is 
moderate (10-15 kt) over the system area and along the predicted path . Similarly, the deep layer 
wind-shear (200-850 hpa) is modera te (15-20 kt) over system area and along the expected path . 
The continuous interaction between these two systems  inhibited further intensification of Arnab. 
Further, the vertical wind shear is becoming high over Andhra Pradesh & adjoining land areas. It 
would lead to rapid weakening of the system after landfall. 
 
The system is trailing along the outer periphery of t he upper sub-tropical ridge (STR) near 20°N. A 
deep trough in westerly is seen along 67 °E to the north of 15 °N at 200 hPa level. It is expected to 
reach 80°E on 28th. It would restrict the westward movement of the system after landfall and would 
also support northwards re-curvature of the system after landfall. 
 
The Total Precipitable water (TPW) indicates warm moist air (75mm) incursion into the system 
area from south and southeast. Cold dry air incursion is also seen in the  western periphery which 
may lead to slight weakening  of the system  at the time of landfall . The dry cold air over the 
Andhra, Odisha and Chhattisgarh region will lead to rapid weakening of the system after landfall
