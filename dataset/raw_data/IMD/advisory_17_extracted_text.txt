Sub: Very Severe Cyclonic Storm ―Arnab‖ [Pronunciation: Ornab] ove r Westcentral Bay of 
Bengal close to Andhra Pradesh coast [Post Landfall Outlook for Andhra Pradesh and 
adjoining South Odisha coasts—Red Message] 
 
The very severe cyclonic storm “Arnab” [Pronunciation :Ornab] ove r Westcentral Bay of Bengal 
close to Andhra Pradesh coast remains practically stationary during past 03 hours, and lay 
centered at 0000 UTC of today, the 28th October, 2026 over the same region, near latitude 16.4°N 
and longitude 82.0°E, about 30 km east of Narsapur (43187),  70 km south-southwest of Kakinada 
(43189), 90 km east -northeast of Machilipatnam (43185),  and 170 km east -northeast of Bapatla 
(43220). 
 
It is very likely to continue to move  northwestwards and cross Andhra Pradesh & Yanam coasts 
between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, close to south of Yanam as a very severe cyclonic storm 
with the max imum sustained wind speed of 115 -125 kmph gusting to 140 kmph during next 2  
hours. 
 
Very severe c yclonic storm “Arnab” is continuing to interact with the land surface and the spiral 
band of northern sector has further entered into the land.  Centre of system is very close to the 
coast line and the landfall process will continue during next 2 hours.

As per latest DWR Machilipatnam imagery at 00 02 UTC, there is a significant reduction in 
convection in wall cloud region especially in eastern and northern sector . Eye is not seen  at 0002 
UTC. The outer spiral band in the northern sector is interacting with the land surface. All these 
signal further weakening of the system.  
 
The intensity of the system is characterized as T4. 0. Associated scattered to broken, low and 
medium clouds with embedded intense to very intense convection lay over the northwest, central 
& south Bay of Bengal and North Coastal Andhra Pradesh, southeast Bihar, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, 
Jharkhand, Gangetic West Bengal, East Madhya Pradesh,  and Eas t Telangana. Minimum Cloud

Bay of Bengal and west Telangana, rest Bihar and Southeast Uttar Pradesh, Rayalaseema, West 
Telangana (Minimum Cloud Top Temperature (CTT) was -40 to -70°C).  
 
The estimated central pressure is 9 85 hPa and maximum sustained wind speed  (MSW) is 65 kt 
gusting to 75 kt.

Wind Warning:  
Gale wind speed reaching 115-125 gusting to 140 kmph is prevailing and is very likely to decrease 
gradually and becoming 80-90 gusting to 1 00 kmph by 0600 UTC  of 28th October 2026. After 
landfall, it would start decreasing becoming gale wind speed re aching 55-65 gusting to 75 kmph 
by 1200 UTC of 28th October, squally wind speed reaching 30-40 gusting to 55 kmph by 0000 
UTC of 29th October and decrease gradually thereafter.  
 
Storm Surge Warning:  
 
Storm surge of 1.5 to 2.5 m height above astronomical t ide is very likely to inundate low lying 
areas of Andhra Pradesh during the time of landfall. The maximum peak surge is expected over 
near Kakinada and Yanam (Puducherry).  Surge height of 2 -3.5 m is expected along the Godavari 
river mouth. (Figure attached).

Sea Condition: 
 
Sea condition is likely to be Phenomenal till landfall (forenoon of 28th October). After landfall, it 
would start improving becoming very high to high by evening of 28th October and improve further 
becoming very rough to rough by morni ng of 29th October and further improve gradually 
thereafter. 
 
Remarks – 
The guidance from various numerical models indicates that the Madden Julian Oscillation (MJO) is 
currently in Phase 3 with amplitude more than 1. It is likely to continue in same phase  during next 
5 days with amplitude more than 1. Thus, MJO is favorable for enhancement of convective activity 
and further intensification of “Arnab” till landfall over the Bay of Bengal (BoB). 
  
The guidance from the NCICS model indicates  prevalence of westerly wind anomaly (7 -9mps) 
along with simultaneous occurrence of Equatorial Rossby wave (ERW),   Kelvin wave (KW) & 
Madden Julian Oscillation ( MJO) wave over south and adjoining central BoB and easterly wind 
anomaly (7-9 mps) over north BoB. These features  indicate a highly favorable environment for its 
further intensification of system till 28th Oct.  
 
The Sea surface temperature is 30°C over eastcentral and adjoining Westcentral BoB and around  
29°C along the east coast of India.  The ocean heat content is  around 80-100 KJ/cm 2 over most 
parts of Bay of Bengal with 100 -120 KJ/cm 2 over some pockets over southwest and adjoining 
westcentral Bay of Bengal. 
 
The positive low -level vorticity is around 200 X10-6 s-1 to the south of system area  with vertical 
extension upto 200 hPa. Another zone of positive vorticity of 100 X10-6 s-1 is seen to its southeast, 
in association with the upper air cyclonic circulation over southwe st BoB. The low level vorticity is 
circularly oriented and is interacting with the vorticity a ssociated with the upper air cyclonic 
circulation over southwest Bay of Bengal.  The low level convergence  is around 30X10-6 s-1 near 
the southeast of system centre . It is extending upto southeast Bay of Bengal . Upper-level 
divergence is around 40X10-6 s-1 to the southeast of system centre. Mid-level wind-shear (500-850 
hpa) is moderate (15-20 kt) over the system area and High along the predicted path. Similarly, the 
deep layer wind-shear (200-850 hpa) is moderate (15-20 kt) over system area and High along the 
expected path. Further, the vertical wind shear is becoming high over Andhra Pradesh & adjoining 
land areas. It would lead to rapid weakening of the system after landfall. 
 
The system is trailing along the outer periphery of t he upper sub-tropical ridge (STR) near 20°N. A 
deep trough in westerly is seen along 67 °E to the north of 15 °N at 200 hPa level. It is expected to 
reach 80°E on 28th. It would restrict the westward movement of the system after landfall and would 
also support northwards re-curvature of the system after landfall. 
 
The Total Precipitable water (TPW) indicates warm mo ist air (70 mm) incursion into the system 
area from south and southeast. Cold dry air incursion is also seen in the western periphery which 
may lead to slight weakening  of the system  at the time of landfall . The dry cold air over the 
Andhra, Odisha and Chha ttisgarh region will lead to rapid weakening of the system after landfall 
on 28th when the system moves across these areas.  
 
Most of the models  are indicating to recurve northwestwards and almost northward movement 
after landfall. IMD MME  is al so indicati ng similar movement. There is large divergence among 
various models with respect to intensification. Peak intensity is varying between 50-70kt. However, 
latest observations indicate that  landfall process has commence d and it will continue another 2 
hours time.
